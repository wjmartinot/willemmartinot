Executive Portrait Photography: What to Look For

An executive portrait is not an ordinary headshot. The image of a CEO, board member or member of the management team appears on the website, in the annual report, alongside press releases and on LinkedIn — sometimes for years. It is the first thing a journalist, potential partner or new client sees before any conversation has taken place.

The choice of photographer shapes how your organisation and its leadership present themselves externally. These are the five criteria that matter.

1. Experience with directors and executives

Photographing a board member is different from photographing a staff member. Executives have limited time, are accustomed to projecting authority but rarely feel comfortable in front of a lens, and they have a clear sense of how they want to come across — even if they cannot always articulate it.

An experienced portrait photographer guides calmly and clearly, keeps the session as efficient as possible, and knows how to draw out a natural, authoritative presence without making it feel posed. Ask to see previous executive portraits in the portfolio and look specifically at whether the images feel authentic — neither stiff nor too casual.

2. Location: office, studio or outdoors?

The setting of an executive portrait says something about the organisation. Three options, each with its own advantages and considerations.

At the executive's own office provides a contextual background that reflects the position and organisational culture. The challenge: lighting conditions are rarely ideal and require experience to handle well.

In a neutral studio environment produces consistent, timeless images that work across all channels — from website to annual report. Ideal when several executives need to be photographed in a unified style that matches the organisation's visual identity.

At a representative location in the city — a recognisable building, a distinctive environment — works well for personal branding and LinkedIn, but requires more time and preparation.

Discuss in advance how the images will be used. That determines which setting fits best.

3. Consistency across multiple executives

Organisations with a board of directors or a broader management team need images that work as a set — without each portrait being photographed in a different style. This requires a photographer who has a consistent working method and can maintain that visual consistency across multiple sessions and even multiple years.

Ask whether the photographer can document the technical parameters — lighting, background, framing — so that a new executive can be added to the set at a later date in the same style.

4. Speed and practical planning

A shoot for a single executive typically takes between 30 and 60 minutes. For a full management team, an efficient rotation works best — with everyone photographed one after another without the organisation losing half a day.

Ask about the possibility of receiving a first selection of images the same day for immediate use. For press moments, announcements or website updates, fast delivery is often essential.

5. Usage rights and licensing

An executive portrait is used across multiple channels: website, LinkedIn, annual report, press releases, internal communications. Make sure the usage agreement is clearly documented. May the image be used without restriction for all internal and external communications? And for how long?

A professional photographer works with a clear written confirmation covering scope, licensing and delivery — without surprises afterwards.

Practical: what to include in your enquiry

The more context you provide, the better a photographer can advise on the right approach. Useful information:

  • How many people need to be photographed?
  • Location preference: office, studio or outdoors?
  • Primary use: website, LinkedIn, annual report, or all of the above?
  • Is there a house style or reference portrait the images should match?
  • What is the required delivery timeline?

With this information you will receive a tailored quote and a concrete proposal for the approach.
