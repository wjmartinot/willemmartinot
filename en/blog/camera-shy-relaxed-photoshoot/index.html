Camera shy? How a relaxed photoshoot works

Many people who come to my studio say it as soon as they walk in: “I don’t really like being photographed.” That’s fine. Helping with that is part of my job.

“Even though I don’t usually like being photographed”

Marit was looking for a corporate portrait photographer and found my website via Google. After the shoot she left a review:

“He created a warm, relaxed atmosphere and the shoot felt very natural. Even though I don’t usually like being photographed, I really enjoyed it — and the photos turned out beautifully too!”

You can see that ease in the image: an open expression, a light smile, shoulders that aren’t raised.

You can’t add that afterwards in Photoshop. It has to be there during the shoot.

You’re not alone — even if you lead a team

You might assume a CEO, board member or lawyer has no trouble with a camera. They address rooms, negotiate and make big decisions. Yet I often see the same tension with them.

That makes sense. In a meeting you have control. In front of a camera you don’t: you don’t know how you look, what to do with your hands or where to look. And the result ends up on your website, in the annual report or on LinkedIn, where everyone can see it.

So the awkwardness rarely sits in the person. It sits in the situation. And that situation is something I can change.

How I put you at ease

  1. Talk first, photograph later. We start with a cup of tea and a conversation about what you need the photos for. The camera stays on the table.
  2. You don’t have to “just act natural”. That works for nobody. I give concrete directions: how you sit, where your hands go, where you look.
  3. Build up slowly. The first frames are to get used to each other. Only then come the images you’ll actually use.
  4. Review together. Along the way I show you the photos. Once you see it’s going well, you relax almost automatically.
  5. No rush. A shoot is only done when we’re both happy.

What you can do yourself

  • Choose clothes you feel comfortable in. A suit that pinches or a stiff new shirt shows up in your posture.
  • Don’t schedule a meeting right after the shoot. Watching the clock is the opposite of relaxing.
  • Just say it. “I find this nerve-racking” is useful information for me, not a confession.

Nervous? You’re in the right place

Marit didn’t like being photographed and ended up enjoying the shoot. That’s exactly what I aim for: that you’re happy with the result and with how it came about.
